About

Call me a Sports Pracademic.

I have spent more than 20 years on both sides of sport: building programs on the ground and studying the systems behind them. I see sport as one of the most powerful tools we have to form people and grow communities. I also see what sports takes from athletes when no one is watching.

That is why my work sits at the intersection of NIL, athlete entrepreneurship, HBCU sport and small-market sports activation. The goal is simple: help athletes, families and organizations compete for the good in life, not just the next contract.

Educator

I teach sport administration and sport business as an Assistant Professor of Instruction at Ohio University, where I also coach students one-on-one on their sports careers and lead international programs, including study abroad in Australia. Before Ohio, I spent seven years at Saint Augustine's University teaching, coordinating the sport management program and serving as the Faculty Athletics Representative to the NCAA.

Researcher

Ed.D. in Sport Leadership and Theory, United States Sports University. My dissertation examined how athletic identity and experience shape entrepreneurial outcomes for college athletes and sport management majors, and it produced the NIL Activity Scale, the foundation of the NIL Readiness Score. I also developed the Six-P Pipeline, a framework for how youth and college sports move athletes from Project to Product, and have written white papers on the economic impact of local sports.

Builder

At Saint Augustine's University I started the first HBCU cycling and women's crew teams in the country, and the cycling program drew coverage from CNN, PBS and Andscape. In Durham, NC, I founded the Bull City Legacy, a semi-professional basketball team. Today I advise organizations through Umar T. Muhammad, LLC.
